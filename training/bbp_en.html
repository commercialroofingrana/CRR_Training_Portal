📋 About This Program

👷

Who It Covers

All CRR employees, supervisors, temporary workers, and subcontractors on jobsites, facilities, shops, yards, or traveling for business.

🏗️

What Work Is Covered

Roof replacement, service, repairs, material handling, emergency response, first aid, and any related jobsite activity.

📅

When Training Is Required

Before covered tasks, when job duties change, after a new hazard is introduced, after an incident or near miss, whenever retraining is needed.

⚖️

Stricter Rule Applies

Customer, general contractor, OSHA, state, and local requirements may be stricter than this program. The stricter requirement always governs.

📜 Reference: OSHA 29 CFR 1910.1030 — Bloodborne Pathogens. This standard protects workers with occupational exposure to blood or other potentially infectious materials (OPIM).

👥 Roles & Responsibilities

🏢

Management

Provide resources, training, equipment, enforcement, and oversight necessary to implement this program.

🦺

Supervisors / Foremen

Plan the work, identify hazards, verify controls are in place, conduct jobsite briefings, stop unsafe work, and document inspections or incidents.

👷

Employees

Follow this program, use assigned PPE correctly, report hazards immediately, and stop work when conditions are unsafe.

🤝

Subcontractors

Comply with CRR requirements, customer and site requirements, OSHA requirements, and provide training documentation when requested.

✋

Stop Work Authority

All employees have the authority and responsibility to stop work if they believe a condition or activity could result in injury, property damage, environmental impact, or a regulatory violation.

⚠️ Work shall NOT resume until the hazard is corrected or controlled. This right is protected — use it without hesitation.

🔬 Exposure Determination

✅ Normally NOT Exposed
🏗️

Routine Roofing Work

Standard roof replacement, repair, and service tasks do not involve blood or bodily fluids.

🔧

Material Handling

Installing materials, handling equipment, and operating tools does not create bloodborne exposure.

⚠️ Potential Exposure Situations
🚑

Emergency Response / First Aid

Responding to an injured coworker may bring you into contact with blood or fluids.

🧹

Cleanup After Injury

Cleaning up blood or contaminated materials at an injury scene creates exposure risk.

💉

Contaminated Sharps

Encountering needles or sharps on a jobsite, abandoned building, or customer facility.

⚠️

Rule

Do NOT handle blood or bodily fluids unless trained, equipped, and directed to do so. Call 911 for serious injuries.

🦠
🧬

🦠 What Are Bloodborne Pathogens?

Bloodborne pathogens are microorganisms found in human blood and certain bodily fluids that can cause disease when they enter another person's body. Even a microscopic, invisible amount can cause infection.
🔴

HIV

Human Immunodeficiency Virus. Attacks the immune system. No cure — managed with lifelong antiretroviral therapy.

🟡

Hepatitis B (HBV)

Attacks the liver. 100× more infectious than HIV via needlestick. A highly effective vaccine is available.

🟠

Hepatitis C (HCV)

Leading cause of liver transplants in the U.S. No vaccine available. New treatments can cure most cases.

🛡️ Universal Precautions

Treat ALL blood and bodily fluids as potentially infectious — regardless of anyone's known health status

🧤

Wear Gloves

Disposable gloves whenever contact with blood or fluids is possible

🥽

Eye & Face Protection

Safety glasses or face shield when splashing is possible

🧼

Wash Hands

Immediately after glove removal or any potential exposure — 20 seconds with soap

🚫

No Food / Drink

Never eat, drink, smoke, or apply cosmetics where exposure may exist

🏷️

Label Biohazards

All contaminated containers must display the ☣️ biohazard symbol

🚨

Avoid Direct Contact

Never touch blood or contaminated materials with bare skin — even briefly

🧤 Personal Protective Equipment

🥽

Eye/Face Protection

Required when splashes or sprays of blood are possible

😷

Surgical Mask

Protects mouth and nose from splatter during cleanup or first aid

🥼

Fluid-Resistant Gown

Worn during significant spill cleanup or first aid procedures

🧤

Disposable Gloves (Latex/Nitrile)

Always worn when contacting blood, OPIM, or contaminated surfaces

👟

Shoe Covers

Used when large-volume spills or soaking contamination is possible

🆘 Exposure Response — Act Immediately

⏰ Every minute counts. Post-exposure treatment must begin within 2 hours to be most effective.
1

Stop Work & Secure Area

Restrict access. Prevent others from contacting the hazard.

2

Wash Immediately

Flush skin with soap and water 15+ min. Flush eyes with clean water 15+ min.

3

Call 911 for Serious Injury

Provide first aid only within your training. Do not attempt advanced care.

4

Notify Supervisor Immediately

Do not delay. Management must arrange post-exposure evaluation and treatment.

5

Seek Medical Evaluation

Confidential evaluation and blood testing guides treatment. PEP must start fast.

6

Document the Incident

Complete an incident report: date, time, location, how exposure occurred, body part affected.

🧹 Cleanup & Disposal Procedure

1
🚨

Alert & Isolate

Warn others, restrict area access, post biohazard warning

2
🧤

Don Full PPE

Gloves, gown, eye protection, and mask before approaching the spill

3
🗞️

Absorb the Spill

Cover with paper towels or absorbent material; work outside-in to avoid spreading

4
🧴

Apply Disinfectant

Use a 1:10 bleach solution or EPA-registered disinfectant; let sit 10 minutes

5
🗑️

Bag & Dispose

All contaminated material goes in a leak-resistant red biohazard bag; dispose per protocol

6
🧼

Remove PPE & Wash Hands

Remove gloves last; wash hands with soap and water for at least 20 seconds

💉 Safe Sharps Handling

✅
DO

Use tongs, forceps, or a dustpan and brush to pick up any sharp objects

🚫
DO NOT

Pick up needles or sharps with bare hands — ever

✅
DO

Place contaminated sharps immediately in an approved puncture-resistant sharps container

🚫
DO NOT

Recap needles by hand — this is the #1 cause of needlestick injuries

✅
DO

Seal sharps containers at the ¾ fill line and label them with the biohazard symbol ☣️

🚫
DO NOT

Attempt to clean up large blood spills unless you are properly trained and equipped

💊 Hepatitis B Vaccination

📋
When employees are assigned duties with reasonably anticipated occupational exposure, management will evaluate vaccination requirements and arrange medical follow-up at no cost to the employee.
💉
The HBV vaccine is a 3-dose series given at 0, 1, and 6 months. It is over 90% effective at preventing Hepatitis B infection.
📝
Employees may decline the vaccine by signing a waiver but may request it at any time thereafter. Vaccination records are kept confidentially by management.
🩸
After completing the series, a titer test confirms immunity. Boosters may be recommended if antibody levels are insufficient.
⚠️
No vaccine exists for HIV or Hepatitis C. PPE and Universal Precautions are your only protection against those pathogens.
